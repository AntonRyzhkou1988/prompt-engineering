## Updated Analysis of Shark Encounter Records for 2017

### A. Factual claims

**Geographic Distribution** (Confidence: High)
- 10 total encounters documented across 5 countries
- USA led with 4 incidents (3 in Florida, 1 in South Carolina)
- Australia had 3 incidents across 3 different states
- Single incidents in Bahamas, United Kingdom, and United Arab Emirates

**Activity Patterns** (Confidence: High)
- Surfing was the most common activity (3 incidents)
- Spearfishing accounted for 2 incidents
- All other activities (body boarding, snorkeling, feeding sharks, fishing, touching shark) had 1 incident each
- Direct interaction activities (feeding sharks, touching shark) comprised 20% of incidents

**Injury Outcomes** (Confidence: High)
- 100% survival rate - all 10 incidents were non-fatal (FatalYN=N)
- 2 incidents resulted in no injury (20%)
- 5 incidents caused minor injuries like lacerations or abrasions (50%)
- 3 incidents resulted in severe injuries including severed forearm and severely bitten leg (30%)

**Species Involvement** (Confidence: Medium)
- Tiger sharks were involved in 2 incidents, both resulting in significant injuries
- White shark (4m) and 7 gill shark incidents both resulted in no injuries
- 50% of records lack complete species identification
- Size data when available ranged from 3 feet to 4 meters

### B. Correlations worth investigating

**Species and Injury Severity** (Confidence: Medium)
- Both identified Tiger shark encounters resulted in severe injuries (forearm severed, arm lacerations)
- Both encounters with identified White shark and 7 gill shark resulted in no injuries
- This pattern suggests potential species-specific risk profiles, though sample size is limited

**Activity Type and Encounter Type** (Confidence: High)
- 8 of 9 typed incidents were unprovoked (88.9%)
- The single provoked incident involved direct touching of the shark
- "Feeding sharks" was classified as unprovoked despite involving direct interaction

**Geographic Clustering** (Confidence: Medium)
- All 3 Florida incidents involved injuries (100% injury rate)
- 2 of 3 Australian incidents resulted in no injury (33% injury rate)
- This regional variation warrants further investigation with larger datasets

### C. Uncertainties or potential biases

**Data Completeness Issues** (Confidence: High)
- 30% of records lack age data
- 50% of records lack time information
- 50% of records lack complete species identification
- 20% of records lack investigator/source documentation
- 1 record lacks incident type classification

**Classification Ambiguities** (Confidence: Medium)
- "Feeding sharks" classified as unprovoked despite deliberate interaction
- Injury severity categories not standardized (e.g., "knocke off board" vs "board bitten" both coded as no injury)
- Species identification includes uncertain qualifiers ("probably a smooth hound")

**Potential Reporting Bias** (Confidence: Medium)
- Minor incidents may be underreported compared to severe injuries
- Geographic coverage may reflect reporting infrastructure rather than actual incident distribution
- English-speaking countries appear overrepresented

### D. Methodological recommendations

**Data Collection Improvements**
1. Implement mandatory fields for time, age, and species to reduce missing data
2. Standardize injury severity scales with clear definitions
3. Create clearer criteria for provoked vs unprovoked classifications, especially for feeding/interaction activities

**Analysis Enhancements**
1. Develop species-specific risk profiles using multi-year data
2. Investigate seasonal patterns once time data completeness improves
3. Cross-reference with local shark population data to assess encounter rates

**Quality Assurance**
1. Establish verification protocols for species identification
2. Implement source documentation standards for all records
3. Create data validation rules to flag inconsistent entries (e.g., feeding sharks as unprovoked)

**Future Research Priorities**
1. Examine why certain species (Tiger sharks) appear associated with more severe injuries
2. Investigate the 100% injury rate in Florida versus other regions
3. Analyze whether "no injury" incidents are increasing due to improved awareness or equipment
